## Technical Writing Style - Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100)

Apply ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) rules to written deliverables: Linear tickets, specs and RFCs, runbooks and docs, PR and commit bodies, and design write-ups. Do not apply STE to conversational replies.

### Rules

- Write short sentences.
- Give one instruction per sentence. Start each instruction with the verb ("Add the secret.", "Deploy the stack.").
- Use the active voice. Use simple tenses only. Do not use continuous or perfect tenses. Do not use "-ing" forms unless they are part of a technical term.
- Use one word for one meaning. Keep terminology consistent. Do not use synonyms for the same thing.
- Keep the articles ("the", "a"). Do not make noun clusters of more than 3 words.
- Prefer plain approved words: "use" not "utilize", "make sure" not "ensure", "about" not "approximately", "before" not "prior to", "start" not "initiate", "if" not "in the event that".
- Put warnings and cautions before the step that they apply to.
- Use vertical lists for complex conditions. Keep each paragraph to one topic.
- The General Guidelines above still apply (objective and concise language, tables for trade-offs).

### Dictionary caveat

- The authoritative, licensed ASD-STE100 dictionary is not available to the agent. Apply the rules above and the common substitutions. Do not reproduce or claim a complete approved-word list.
- Flag any word that could be non-approved. Do not assert certainty.
- If a licensed dictionary, a project term supplement, or an STE checker (for example HyperSTE) is available, conform to it strictly. Software terms (Lambda, secret, shipperId) are technical nouns and are allowed.
